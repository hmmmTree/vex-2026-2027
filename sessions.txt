------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. BRAIN SCREEN NEVER CAME UP (src/odometry.cpp)                     TESTED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- pros::lcd::initialize() was fine. The odometry task was starving the
  display: its three "ignore big jump" guards did a continue with NO
  pros::delay(), so once one tripped the task spun at 100% and LVGL never
  got to draw.
- Three things guaranteed it tripped straight away:
  * Units: threshold was tolerance * inches_per_tick (= 0.00044) compared
    against a delta in CENTIDEGREES. Any movement at all exceeded it.
  * prev_raw_h was compared BEFORE it was ever seeded. While the IMU was
    still calibrating get_heading() returns infinity, so |inf - 0| > 2.5
    was true forever. Locked before "ROBOT READY" could print.
  * A rejected sample never advanced prev_, so one bad read made every
    later read look like the same huge jump.
- Fix: one pros::delay(LOOP_INTERVAL_MS) at the top of the loop so every
  path yields; sensors read once per tick; first good sample only seeds;
  deltas compared in inches against tolerance and in degrees against
  tolerance_heading (which existed in config.hpp but was never used);
  heading delta goes through normalise_angle so 359 -> 1 is not a 358 jump;
  a rejected sample still advances prev_.
- Result: screen came up. User removed the "ROBOT READY" print.

1. PID UNITS WERE WRONG (src/pid.cpp, include/robot/pid.hpp)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Pid::update divided the error change by dt in SECONDS, so with a 20 ms tick
  the derivative came out 50x larger than any tuning guide assumes. Every kd
  in the project was effectively 50x too strong, which is why "increase kd
  when it oscillates" made things worse every single time.
- Changed to per-tick units: the derivative is now the error change over one
  LOOP_INTERVAL_S tick, and the integral adds one error sample per tick. A
  `ticks` factor corrects a loop that ran long.                       TESTED
- All call sites used the default dt, so nothing else needed changing.
- Consequence: every kd in main.cpp had to be re-tuned from scratch.

4. TRACKING WHEEL OFFSETS - THE MAIN RESULT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Symptom: the pose drifted several inches during any turn, cordon circled
  its target, and nothing downstream could be tuned on top of it.
- FIRST APPROACH (fitting the offsets from pose drift after 90/180/270/360
  turns) WASTED HOURS AND GAVE CONTRADICTORY ANSWERS. Two lessons:
  * A constant offset error CANCELS EXACTLY over a full revolution. A 360
    that comes back to zero proves nothing. Only partial angles inform.
  * Successive fits implied physically impossible offsets (9 in, then 19 in)
    because the pose integration was itself being corrupted.
- WHAT ACTUALLY WORKED: a temporary auton that reads the rotation sensors
  DIRECTLY (xrot/yrot get_position before and after), pivots under power, and
  accumulates the true rotation from the IMU:
      x_off =  (dx * inches_per_tick) / total_rad
      y_off = -(dy * inches_per_tick) / total_rad
  Immune to the odometry task, its tolerances, and the current offsets.
- Five runs at two pivot speeds, 30.9 revolutions in total:          TESTED
      v50  1543.9 deg   x -4.417   y 0.622
      v50  1550.7 deg   x -4.338   y 0.632
      v50  1546.3 deg   x -4.357   y 0.638
      v35  3239.9 deg   x -4.349   y 0.630
      v35  3236.2 deg   x -4.335   y 0.636
      pooled            x -4.354   y 0.632
  Speed made no difference, which ruled out speed-dependent skid.
- Set x_tracker_offset = -4.37, y_tracker_offset = 0.63.
- VERIFIED with a second temporary auton that seeds the pose to 0 and samples
  it at 90 / 180 / 270 / 360 of a slow powered pivot. Three runs, every
  quarter turn within 0.2 in on both axes.                           TESTED
  An earlier 5-inch failure of this test turned out to be a stale build, so
  the verifier was changed to print the offsets actually in use.
- Residual drift after calibration: about 0.1 in per full rotation. That is
  real skid and quantisation, not an offset error, and has no knob.
- Both temporary autons were removed afterwards and main.cpp was restored
  byte-identical from a backup.
- CHANGED SINCE: the offsets are now -0.02 / 0.28, because the tracking
  wheels were later found to be FLIPPED (SESSION 2026-10-11, section 4). The
  measurement method above is still the one that works and is now a
  permanent auton ("tune offsets").

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. tolerance_heading WAS THROWING AWAY REAL MOTION (config.hpp)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Was 5 degrees per 20 ms tick, which is 250 deg/s. A powered pivot exceeds
  that, and when the check fires the odometry task discards the WHOLE update
  for that tick, position included. Hand tests never hit it, powered autons
  always did.
- Raised to 30.                                                      TESTED
- This is the most likely reason the early pose-drift fits disagreed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. CORDON COULD ONLY EVER DRIVE FORWARDS (src/drive.cpp)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- drive_power came from hypot(), always positive, and was scaled by
  max(MIN_HEADING_SCALE, cos(turn_error)). With the floor at 0.15 the robot
  still crept forward at 15 percent while facing 180 degrees away from the
  target. Forward motion plus a hard turn is a circle, so it orbited and
  never reached the exit radius.
- The floor was set to 0.0 first (the robot then pivots instead of circling),
  then the clamp was REMOVED entirely so the cosine can go negative and the
  robot simply reverses into the point.                              TESTED
- Effect, same test either side of the change:
      with clamp:  flips 15  maxerr 120  mindst 2.1  period 1069  TIMEOUT
      without:     flips 10  maxerr 102  mindst 2.5  period 1096  SETTLED
- MIN_HEADING_SCALE is now unused.
- CHANGED SINCE: removing the clamp turned out to be a real bug. Past 90 deg
  of error the negative cosine made the robot drive AWAY from the point,
  self-reinforcing. The clamp was put back the next day as
  max(0.0, cos(...)), so the robot pivots instead (SESSION 2026-09-18/19,
  section 2).

7. CORDON OSCILLATION TELEMETRY (src/drive.cpp, temporary)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Added inside cordon and printed on brain lines 3-6 after the motion ends:
  ticks, sign flips of turn_error, max |turn_error|, min distance_left,
  oscillation period, and "aim swing" (the range of desired_heading).
- "aim swing" settled a long argument. It separates the robot swinging around
  a steady target (a gain problem) from the robot faithfully following a
  target that is itself swinging (a geometry problem). Period does the same
  job: under ~100 ms is the derivative chasing noise, ~1 s is a body-sized
  loop, and the two have opposite fixes.
- Worth re-adding whenever cordon misbehaves. Guessing from the outside cost
  far more uploads than the telemetry did.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. BOOMERANG LEAD - WHAT WAS LEARNED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Lead 0 drove cleanly. Everything above 0 oscillated near the target.
- Cause: the carrot sits between the robot and the target, roughly
  distance x (1 - lead) away, so a bigger lead puts the aim point CLOSER to
  the robot and the bearing to it swings harder. CARROT_EPSILON_IN is the
  guard for this and was 0.1, far too small; raised to 4.0 then.      TESTED
- Measurements, same target, final_heading 20:
      lead 0.65   aim swing  71   maxerr 102   ended H   7  (timeout)
      lead 0.4    aim swing  49   maxerr  46   ended H  44
      lead 0.5 A  aim swing  76   maxerr  95   ended H -21  (settled)
      lead 0.5 B  aim swing 186   maxerr 180   ended H  49  (settled)
- THE KEY FINDING: position repeated to within an inch across runs, the final
  heading did NOT. Two identical runs finished 70 degrees apart. cordon never
  closes a loop on heading, it aims at a point, so the heading you end on is
  whatever phase of the swing you were in when you crossed the exit radius.
  No gain or lead value fixes that.
- Dead ends, both made it worse:
  * steering kp 3 -> 1.5: under-steers, cannot follow the curve, circles.
  * halving cordon's drive gains: with less forward power the same steering
    command turns the robot much harder, so it over-rotates. What matters is
    the RATIO of turn_power to drive_power, and 3 : 6 was already about right.
- CHANGED SINCE: CARROT_EPSILON_IN is now 1.5, and 2026-10-11 added a proper
  near-end fix (BOOMERANG_CLOSE_IN) that steers for final_heading inside
  7.5 in (SESSION 2026-10-11, section 8). Same idea, better implemented.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. FINAL HEADING - USE THE PIVOT, NOT THE BOOMERANG
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Conclusion of section 8: the boomerang gets the position, a final pivot
  gets the heading. turn_to is the only thing that closes a loop on heading,
  and it will not exit until the error is under TURN_SETTLE_DEG (0.5) for
  100 ms.
- Set .turn_at_end = true and .final_turn_gains = TURN_GAINS in the request.
  Designated initialisers must be in declaration order or it will not build:
  final_turn_gains goes after turn_gains, turn_at_end after stop_at_end.
- Leaving final_turn_gains unset while turn_at_end is true gives an all-zero
  controller: the pivot commands zero power and burns the whole turn_timeout.
- THE FIRST ATTEMPT OVERSHOT BADLY. Cause: cordon called stop() and turn_to()
  back to back, so the pivot started while the robot was still rolling, and a
  fresh Pid has zero derivative on its first tick, blind to the rotation
  already in progress. Added an explicit stop() plus a 200 ms settle before
  the pivot.                                                    NOT RETESTED
- If the pivot reports timeout it is stalling short: TURN_GAINS kp 0.6 gives
  about 0.3 percent motor power at 0.5 deg of error, not enough to break
  static friction. Raise kp to ~2, or add ki ~0.02 (the integral zone is
  already configured, ki is 0).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. TUNING ORDER THAT CAME OUT OF ALL THIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
      1. tracker offsets      full turn drifts under 0.5 in
      2. straight drive       24 in, ends where you aimed
      3. pivot turns          lands on heading, no hunting
      4. cordon steering      lead 0, merges on line, no weaving
      5. exit radius          parks cleanly, never orbits
      6. boomerang lead       arrives on final heading
- Each stage sits on the one above it. Almost every problem in this session
  traced back to stage 1.
- ONE NUMBER PER UPLOAD. Every time this went sideways it was because two
  things changed between runs and there was no way to tell which did it.
- Symptom -> knob:
      weaves down the path          lower steering kp
      lazy arc, never lines up      raise steering kp
      pivots instead of driving     steering kp far too high
      circles the target            raise exit radius
      hunts only when lead is on    raise carrot epsilon
      shuttles back and forth       let drive power go negative
      arrives facing wrong way      raise lead, or use turn_at_end

4. OSCILLATION HUNT - WHAT IT ACTUALLY WAS                       TESTED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Symptom: big back-and-forth swings on the approach to the final point.
Wrong guesses, in order, all ruled out by measurement:
    stick-slip in the trailing turn, IMU sensor lag, actuator lag,
    sampling beat between the two 20 ms loops, heading signal jumping.
- A kinematic sim reproduced NONE of them, because it was modelling the old
  (clamped) cos and perfectly clean sampling.
- Two measurements were corrupted by the instrumentation itself:
    * a printf inside the control loop stretched the tick to 24.3 ms
    * "rate" divided by a hardcoded 20 ms even when a retried odometry read
      put 40-60 ms between samples, inflating 250 deg/s to ~750
- The number that settled it: "aim 90 jmp 125 avg 299" with rdfail 0 and a
  clean 20.0 ms/tick. avg 299 deg/s is the MEAN turn rate over 8 s - about
  2400 deg of rotation, at the drivetrain's top pivot rate, for one attempt
  to reach a point 5 in away.
- Cause: with CORDON_TURN_GAINS kp 3.0, turn_power passes 100 at only
  33 deg of error. After the "peak > 100" normalisation that is a full-speed
  opposing-wheel pivot with the drive contribution gone - so above 33 deg
  there is NO proportional control, it is bang-bang, and it overshoots by
  construction. kd cannot damp a saturated loop.
- FIX that worked: CORDON_TURN_GAINS kp 3.0 -> 0.6 (the same kp as
  TURN_GAINS, already tuned for pivoting in place). kd 6 -> 5.
- Also added heading.set_derivative_smoothing(CORDON_HEADING_SMOOTHING) with
  CORDON_HEADING_SMOOTHING = 3. The distance PID always had smoothing; the
  heading PID was running on raw per-tick differences.
- These gains were committed on 2026-09-20 as f7b3403 "Tuned constants for
  drive, turn, cordon turn, and lead" and are still the values in
  autons.hpp at 2026-10-11.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

11. SUBSYSTEM AT / BETWEEN WAYPOINTS - DESIGN NOTES          NOT IMPLEMENTED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Question was: how do I move a subsystem between the waypoints of a path.
  The answer covered three levels. The first two turned out to already be
  built (section 9); the answer had been written against the code as it
  stood on 2026-09-19, before autons.cpp / intake.cpp / lift.cpp existed.
- Level 1, split the path: run the approach, call the subsystem, run the
  rest. No new code. Gets unreadable past two or three actions because the
  path stops being one table.
- Level 2, one hook per point: a std::function on each step, fired after
  the point settles. This is exactly what .on_start / .at_end /
  .pause_after already do. Worth keeping from the discussion: the function
  must NOT live on CordonRequest itself (it is passed by value into
  cordon() and would drag <functional> into drive.hpp), and a point that
  times out must not fire its action or you clamp on empty air after a
  failed approach. Both already correct in the code.
- If a subsystem is ever added from scratch: ports + device member in
  include/robot/hardware.hpp, ctor init list in src/hardware.cpp, member on
  Robot - and the declaration-order comment in robot.hpp matters, odometry_
  before anything that takes Odometry& (what Lift does now).
- Level 3, moving a subsystem WHILE the robot drives, is still open.
  Confirmed why: Lift::toggle() (lift.cpp:29) and Lift::drop() (lift.cpp:53)
  are blocking while(true) loops on pros::delay(20) that return only when
  the lift arrives or times out, so calling either from .on_start or
  .at_end parks the drive for the whole lift motion. Lift::move_to() is
  declared in lift.hpp and has no body at all. .on_start is NOT concurrent.
- Three ways looked at. None were written, none compiled, none run:
  * pros::Task holding the subsystem state machine, ticking every 20 ms
    while cordon() drives. Simplest if the subsystem has real state (run
    until a sensor says stop, then reverse). MUST sit at a lower priority
    than the odometry task or it starves the tracker again - the same
    failure fixed in c21625f (SESSION 2026-09-05, section 1).
  * An Actions class built next to Interrupts: same shape (predicate +
    callback + fire-once), polled from the poll site already in cordon's
    loop (src/drive.cpp:273). This is the one that lets a subsystem fire
    partway along a segment on a pose test ("start intake once y > -40")
    instead of only at a point.
  * Blocking at a point, i.e. what section 9 already does. Fine for the
    gripper, useless for a lift that should rise while the robot moves.
- Do NOT do it with an Interrupts predicate's side effect. A predicate
  returning true aborts the motion, and one that does work and returns
  false reads as a bug to whoever opens interrupt.cpp next.
- Two traps checked against the current code, both already handled, no
  change needed - just use the existing tools:
  * A bare pros::delay inside .on_start/.at_end stops Interrupts being
    polled, so driver takeover and the >55 C cut-off go deaf for that
    window. ctx.wait() (src/autons.cpp:460) and .pause_after already poll;
    use them instead of pros::delay anywhere inside a path.
  * .on_start on a pass-through point (stop_at_end false, exit_radius
    WAYPOINT_RADIUS 6) fires while the robot is still rolling several
    inches short of the point. Right for intake().run, wrong for anything
    that blocks - the robot coasts through it.

2. SCORING  (manual v1.0, rules <SC1>-<SC6>)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Point table:
      Autonomous Bonus             12 points
      Each Scored Alliance Pin      5 points
      Each Scored yellow Pin       10 points
      Each Robot in the Midfield    8 points

- <SC2> PLACED. A Pin is Placed if it is partly or entirely nested with a
  Goal, or with a Cup that is itself nested with another Placed Pin - AND
  each Goal / Cup half it nests with holds at most one Pin half.
- <SC3> SCORED. A Pin has two halves and can have 0, 1 or 2 Scored halves.
  A half only Scores if it stays fully visible, i.e. NOT partly or fully
  inside the OPAQUE (gray) half of a Cup. Inside the clear half still counts.
      a. each visible red half    ->  5 to red
      b. each visible blue half   ->  5 to blue
      c. each visible yellow half -> 10 to whoever Owns that Pin
  So scoring is PER HALF, not per Pin. A yellow/yellow Pin that is Placed,
  Owned and fully visible is worth 20.
- <SC4> A Toggle only counts as set to a colour if it is fully seated flat
  against its mount AND not being touched by a Robot. Otherwise it is
  neutral (yellow) by default.
- <SC5> OWNERSHIP of yellow halves:
      in a Quadrant  - the Alliance that Quadrant's Toggle is set to.
                       Toggle on yellow = yellow there scores nothing.
      in the Midfield- whoever ends the Match with MORE Robots in the
                       Midfield. Equal count = Midfield yellow scores
                       nothing for either Alliance.
- <SC6> A Robot is "in the Midfield" if ANY part of it is inside the infinite
  vertical projection of the Midfield at the end of the Match.
- The manual's own worked example (one stack, three Toggle states) - use this
  to check the scoring function before trusting it:
      Toggle yellow  ->  red 15 / blue  5
      Toggle blue    ->  red 15 / blue 35
      Toggle red     ->  red 45 / blue  5

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. AUTON BONUS AND AWP  (<SC7>, <SC8>)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Auton Bonus is 12 to whoever is ahead when auton ends. A tie, including
  0-0, gives 6 to each. Midfield points are NOT counted when comparing auton
  scores. Any violation in auton hands the bonus to the other Alliance;
  if both violate, nobody gets it.
- AWP needs all three, with no violations:
      1. at least 7 Pins Scored for you
      2. at least 3 Goals each holding at least 2 Pins Scored for you
      3. neither Robot touching the Field Perimeter
  1 and 2 ignore anything in Quadrants on the opponent's side of the
  Autonomous Line.
- Q&A 3179 (2026-06-30): "Pins Scored for your Alliance" INCLUDES owned
  yellow Pins, not just red/blue ones. 5 blue + 2 owned yellow = 7, valid.
- The manual says the 2026-09-03 update makes AWP harder for events that
  qualify straight to Worlds (its example: 8 Pins and/or 4 Goals). We never
  found a manual newer than 1.0, so that wording is UNCONFIRMED. Build with
  7 / 3 as the default and keep those two numbers easy to change.
